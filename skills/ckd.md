# Skill: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
### Contributor(s): Paulius Mui, MD; Virginia Irwin-Scott, DO, MBA, FAIM, FASN, CPE, FACOI

## History
- CKD history: Known duration, baseline creatinine/eGFR, rate of progression, and prior nephrology involvement.
- Etiology: Diabetes, hypertension, glomerular disease, hereditary disease (e.g. PKD), obstructive/urologic disease, or unknown.
- Proteinuria history: Prior UACR/UPCR results.
- Prior kidney injury: AKI episodes, contrast exposure, nephrolithiasis, recurrent UTIs, or obstructive uropathy.
- Diet: Sodium intake, protein intake, hydration habits, and high-potassium/high-phosphorus foods when relevant.
- NSAID use or frequent OTC analgesic use.
- Activity: Exercise tolerance and physical activity level.
- Occupational/environmental exposures: Heavy metals, solvents, heat stress, dehydration risk, or nephrotoxic exposures.

## Focused ROS
- Constitutional: Fatigue, poor appetite, weight change, or decreased exercise tolerance.
- GU: Hematuria, foamy urine, nocturia, dysuria, weak stream, urinary retention, polyuria, oliguria, or decreased urine output.
- Cardiorespiratory: Dyspnea, orthopnea, chest pain, palpitations, or exercise intolerance.
- GI: Nausea or metallic taste.
- Edema/Volume: Leg swelling, abdominal distention, or rapid weight gain.
- Neurologic: Restless legs, peripheral neuropathy, sleep disturbance, muscle cramps, cognitive changes, or confusion.
- Bone/Mineral: Bone pain, fractures, or proximal muscle weakness.
- Skin/Heme: Pruritus, pallor, easy bruising, or excoriations.

## Social History
- Tobacco, alcohol, and substance use.
- Barriers to care: Cost, transportation, medication access, or health literacy.

## Family History
- CKD, dialysis, or kidney transplant in first-degree relatives.
- Polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD/ARPKD).
- Alport syndrome or other hereditary nephropathies.
- Diabetes, hypertension, or premature cardiovascular disease.
- Autoimmune disease with renal involvement.

## Medical History
- Diabetes.
- Hypertension.
- Cardiovascular disease: CAD, heart failure, stroke, or PAD.
- Glomerular disease or nephrotic syndrome.
- Nephrolithiasis or recurrent urinary tract infections.
- BPH, urinary retention, or structural urinary tract disease.
- Autoimmune disease (e.g. lupus, vasculitis).
- Prior AKI.
- Gout or hyperuricemia.
- Liver disease or chronic viral infections (HBV, HCV, HIV).

## Surgeries
- Kidney or urinary tract procedures: Stents, nephrostomy, lithotripsy, or nephrectomy.
- Prostate or pelvic surgery affecting urinary outflow.
- Vascular access placement: AV fistula, AV graft, or peritoneal dialysis catheter.
- Kidney/pancreas transplant history.
- Bariatric surgery (affects nutrition and stone risk).
- Parathyroidectomy (for severe hyperparathyroidism).

## Hospitalizations
- AKI hospitalization, or AKI requiring dialysis or other renal replacement therapy.
- Decompensated heart failure or volume overload.
- Hyperkalemia, severe metabolic acidosis, or uremic complications.
- Nephrolithiasis, pyelonephritis, urinary obstruction, or hypertensive emergency.

## OTC
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, celecoxib).
- Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs).
- Herbal supplements or bodybuilding supplements.
- Creatine supplements.
- Magnesium- or aluminum-containing antacids.
- Phosphate-containing laxatives or enemas.
- OTC decongestants and sodium-containing antacids.

## Exam
- Weight and volume status: Edema, JVP, crackles, ascites, or dry weight changes.
- Cardiac: Murmurs, S3/S4, pericardial friction rub, or signs of heart failure.
- Abdomen: Bladder distention, palpable kidneys, flank masses, or bruits.
- Skin: Pallor, excoriations/pruritus, bruising, or uremic changes.
- Neuro: Asterixis, mental status change, or peripheral neuropathy.
- Vascular: Peripheral pulses, PAD findings, and dialysis access thrill/bruit if applicable.

## Common Comorbidities
- Hypertension.
- Diabetes.
- Cardiovascular disease / heart failure / PAD.
- Hyperlipidemia.
- Obesity.
- Anemia of CKD.
- CKD-MBD (mineral and bone disorder).
- Metabolic acidosis.
- Hyperkalemia.
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism.
- Immunosuppression.

## Cardiac Diagnostics
- EKG: Hyperkalemia changes, LVH, arrhythmia, or prior ischemia.
- Echocardiogram: Heart failure, structural disease, cardiomyopathy, or pericardial effusion.

## Labs
- CMP.
- CBC.
- Phosphorus.
- Magnesium.
- Urine albumin: UACR.
- Urinalysis with microscopy.
- A1c.
- Lipid panel.
- Iron studies if anemia present.
- PTH and 25-OH vitamin D when CKD-MBD is suspected.
- Cystatin C (when available/appropriate to refine GFR estimate).
- Uric acid when clinically relevant.
- Serologic workup when glomerular/secondary cause is suspected:
  - ANA, anti-dsDNA, complements (C3, C4, CH50).
  - ANCA, anti-GBM, PLA2R antibody.
  - SPEP/UPEP (immunoglobulins, serum and urine immunofixation, serum free kappa/lambda ratio).
  - Hepatitis panel, HIV.

## Imaging
- Renal ultrasound (kidney size, echogenicity, cortical thinning, hydronephrosis, cysts, obstruction).
- Bladder scan/post-void residual if retention suspected.
- Non-contrast CT for stones or structural disease when indicated.
- Renal artery Doppler or CT/MR angiography if renovascular disease is suspected.

## Preventive Care
- Avoid nephrotoxins and unnecessary NSAIDs.
- Sodium restriction counseling.
- Protein intake counseling when appropriate for CKD stage and nutrition status.
- Weight management and exercise counseling.
- Smoking cessation counseling.
- Sick day guidance for dehydration-prone patients on kidney-active medications.
- Contrast safety planning when imaging/procedures are needed.
- Dietary counseling or renal dietitian referral for potassium, phosphorus, sodium, and protein management.
- Preserve veins for future dialysis access in advanced CKD when appropriate.

## Immunizations
- Annual influenza vaccine.
- Pneumococcal vaccination when indicated.
- Hepatitis B vaccination when CKD is advanced or dialysis risk is higher.
- COVID vaccination per current recommendations.

## Specialist Co-management
- Nephrology (advanced CKD, heavy albuminuria, rapid progression, unclear cause, or difficult complications).
- Endocrinology (complex diabetes optimization).
- Cardiology (heart failure, CAD, resistant HTN, or cardiorenal syndrome).
- Urology (obstruction, recurrent stones, retention, or structural disease).
- Renal dietitian.
- Transplant evaluation when kidney failure risk is rising or eGFR is severely reduced.

## Risk Calculators
- CKD staging by CGA classification (Cause, GFR category, Albuminuria category).
- KDIGO CKD risk stratification / heat map.
- KFRE (Kidney Failure Risk Equation) when applicable.
- ASCVD risk score.

## Goal-directed Medical Therapy
- Confirm CKD chronicity (persistent abnormality ≥3 months).
- Stage CKD by eGFR (G1–G5) and albuminuria (A1–A3).
- Blood pressure target individualized; commonly <130/80 mmHg when tolerated.
- ACE inhibitor or ARB for albuminuric CKD when indicated.
- SGLT2 inhibitor for eligible CKD patients, especially with albuminuria and/or diabetes.
- Nonsteroidal MRA (e.g. finerenone) for eligible patients with T2DM + CKD + albuminuria.
- Statin therapy when indicated for CKD/CVD risk reduction.
- Correct metabolic acidosis when present.
- Manage hyperkalemia proactively to preserve kidney-protective therapy when possible.
- Individualize glycemic targets in diabetic CKD.
- Nephrology referral for eGFR <30, A3 albuminuria, rapid decline, resistant HTN, persistent electrolyte/acid-base issues, unexplained hematuria, recurrent stones, hereditary disease, or unclear etiology.

## Medications
- ACE inhibitors (lisinopril) or ARBs (losartan).
- SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin).
- Nonsteroidal MRA (finerenone), or spironolactone, in appropriate diabetic CKD.
- Diuretics: Loop diuretics (furosemide, torsemide, bumetanide) for volume overload; thiazide/thiazide-like agents as appropriate.
- Statins.
- Potassium binders when needed to support RAAS therapy.
- Alkali therapy (e.g. sodium bicarbonate) for metabolic acidosis when indicated.
- Iron supplementation when indicated.
- ESA consideration with nephrology for anemia of CKD when appropriate.
- Phosphate binders in advanced CKD when indicated.
- Active vitamin D therapy or calcimimetic therapy in select advanced CKD patients with specialist guidance.

## Wearables
- CGM if diabetes management would benefit.

## Supplies
- Home dialysis.
- Blood pressure cuff.
- Scale for daily weights.
- Pill box / medication organizer.

## Key Targets
- BP <130/80.
- A1c <8.0.
- These are the two reasons most people will progress. Getting the message to them early saves limbs, vision, hearts, and kidneys.
